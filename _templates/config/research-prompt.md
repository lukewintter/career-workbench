# Company Research Brief — Interview Prep Prompt

> **How to use**: Replace `[COMPANY]`, `[ROLE]`, and `[ROLE_TYPE]` before running, then paste the full job ad below the prompt.
> `[ROLE_TYPE]` = a short descriptor of the function or track (e.g. "technical leadership", "commercial leadership", "operations").
> The job ad is a required input — the brief cannot be properly calibrated without it.

## Inputs

- The raw job ad for this application
- The company website (especially offering overview, pricing if applicable, and about pages)
- Recent news or funding announcements if available

## Prompt

Use this (or adapt it as needed) when running deep research with an external tool:

---

You are helping a professional prepare for an application and interview process at [COMPANY] for a [ROLE] position. Frame insights for someone on a [ROLE_TYPE] path where relevant.

The job ad is provided below. Treat it as a primary input — cross-reference it throughout the brief to flag where the company's public signals align with or contradict what they say they're looking for.

Using the job ad, company website, recent news, and any other credible sources you can find, produce a concise research brief using the sections and headings below — in order, exactly as written. For each section, focus on concrete signals rather than generic descriptions. If information is unavailable or unclear, note it as an open question rather than guessing or inferring.

Cite your sources throughout. The areas most prone to hallucination in company research are financial figures, org structure and leadership titles, and commercial or pricing details. If you cannot find a credible source for any of these, say so explicitly rather than estimating.

Use the following labels consistently throughout the brief to distinguish confidence levels:
- **Source:** — found in a specific, credible source (cite it inline)
- **Estimate:** — a rough or unconfirmed figure from a secondary source (e.g. Crunchbase, analyst guess, a range)
- **Inferred:** — not found directly, but reasoned from related signals; state the reasoning
- **Open question:** — nothing found; will not guess

Prioritise signal over completeness. A sharp insight beats a comprehensive list.

---

### Section 1 — Company and market snapshot (high priority)

- Founding story and key evolution milestones.
- Current size (headcount), offices, geographic focus.
- Funding history, key investors, and current stage (or public market status).
- Revenue estimates and growth trajectory over the last 3–5 years (cite sources; label estimates).
- Market category and how the company defines it.
- Competitive landscape and where [COMPANY] positions itself within it.

---

### Section 2 — Recent news and key events (medium priority)

- Significant announcements, launches, or funding events in the last 12 months.
- Any M&A activity, leadership changes, layoffs, or regulatory issues.
- What the news suggests about the company's current trajectory.

---

### Section 3 — Differentiation and defensibility (very high priority)

- What structurally differentiates [COMPANY] from its closest competitors?
- Is differentiation primarily experience-driven, workflow-driven, integration-driven, or technology-driven?
- Evidence of defensibility: network effects, switching costs, data advantages, ecosystem lock-in.
- Honest assessment of commoditisation risk or competitive pressure.

---

### Section 4 — Commercial model and growth levers (high priority)

- Pricing or packaging model if discoverable.
- Primary customer profile: SMB, mid-market, or enterprise? Mixed?
- Key integrations, partnerships, or channel signals.
- Geographic or vertical expansion signals.

---

### Section 5 — Organisation and operating-model signals (high priority)

- Notable executive and functional leadership (names, backgrounds, tenure where credible sources allow).
- Signals of go-to-market motion (e.g. sales-led, self-serve, hybrid) where evidence exists — cite hiring language, packaging, or public statements.
- Cultural and execution signals from public sources: how they describe work, pace of change, published practices, conference content, job descriptions.
- Indicators of organisational maturity vs. purely reactive execution.

---

### Section 6 — Culture and leadership (medium priority)

- Cultural signals from public sources: values language, employee-review themes (with caveats), how they position themselves externally.
- Notable leadership changes in the last 12 months and what they signal.
- Red flags worth noting (executive turnover, funding gaps, strategic pivots, negative press).

---

### Section 7 — People and relationship map (low priority)

Keep this brief. Note the hiring manager if identifiable from public sources, and flag anyone notable in the part of the org relevant to [ROLE_TYPE]. If information is sparse, say so and move on — do not speculate.

---

### Section 8 — Risks and open questions

- The two or three biggest concerns or uncertainties surfaced by the research.
- What is still unclear and could not be resolved from public sources.

---

**Format**: 2–3 page executive briefing. Write in clear, factual prose. Avoid bullet-point padding — if a point isn't concrete, cut it or flag it as an open question.

# Grew expansion revenue with a self-serve team tier

*(Fictional example — Firstname Lastname.)*

## One-liner

Designed and shipped Example Learning's self-serve team tier, opening a bottom-up expansion path that grew expansion revenue 31% in two quarters.

## Situation

- Business/user context: individual learners were quietly using Example Learning at work and asking to add teammates, but the only B2B route was a slow sales-led contract.
- Problem/opportunity: no self-serve way to turn an engaged individual into a paying team — expansion depended entirely on outbound sales.
- Why it mattered: bottom-up expansion is far cheaper to acquire than net-new sales-led logos.

## Task and my role

- Goal: create a self-serve upgrade path from individual to team, and prove it drove incremental revenue.
- Success criteria: a measurable lift in expansion revenue without cannibalising individual subscriptions.
- Constraints: billing system built for single-seat subscriptions; sales wary of channel conflict.
- My mandate / decision rights: owned the packaging and the self-serve upgrade surface; pricing set jointly with finance.
- Owned vs. contributed: owned the tier design, upgrade flow, and rollout; contributed to the pricing decision.
- Users / stakeholders: engaged individual learners, team admins; partners in finance, sales, and customer success.
- Collaboration: 5 engineers, 1 designer; finance and sales as key stakeholders.

## Actions

- Interviewed 12 individual users who had asked about team seats; found the trigger was wanting to share progress with 3–5 colleagues, not a big rollout.
- Designed a lightweight team tier (up to 10 seats, self-serve billing, a shared progress view) deliberately sized below the sales-led threshold.
- Aligned sales by drawing a clear line: self-serve stops at 10 seats, everything above routes to sales — turning channel conflict into a qualified-lead source.
- Built an in-product upgrade prompt triggered when a user invited a colleague.
- Rolled out to 20% of eligible accounts first, then to all once expansion revenue held with no individual-plan cannibalisation.

## Results and impact

- Expansion revenue grew 31% across the two quarters after full rollout.
- The self-serve tier generated a steady flow of qualified >10-seat leads for sales, rather than competing with them.
- No measurable cannibalisation of individual subscriptions.

## Reflection

- What worked: sizing the tier from the real trigger (share with a few colleagues) instead of guessing at a plan.
- What I learned: the packaging boundary was as much an org-alignment decision as a pricing one.
- What I would do differently: instrument the invite→upgrade path more granularly before launch.
- What this says about my strengths/gaps: strong on connecting product to revenue and aligning stakeholders; less tested on large enterprise contracts.

# Cut new-learner activation drop-off with an onboarding redesign

*(Fictional example — Firstname Lastname.)*

## One-liner

Rebuilt Example Learning's new-learner onboarding around the first completed lesson, cutting week-one activation drop-off by 22 points.

## Situation

- Business/user context: Example Learning acquired plenty of sign-ups but too few became habitual learners; growth marketing was filling a leaky bucket.
- Problem/opportunity: only 41% of new learners completed a first lesson in week one — the single strongest predictor of retention.
- Why it mattered: activation was the top constraint on both retention and the paid-conversion funnel downstream.

## Task and my role

- Goal: raise week-one first-lesson completion without adding acquisition spend.
- Success criteria: +10 point lift in first-lesson completion, held in a controlled test.
- Constraints: legacy onboarding flow with significant technical debt; no new headcount.
- My mandate / decision rights: owned the onboarding surface end to end; roadmap and experiment budget mine.
- Owned vs. contributed: owned problem definition, sequencing, and the experiment design; engineering and design owned implementation.
- Users / stakeholders: new learners; partners in growth marketing and data.
- Collaboration: 4 engineers, 1 designer, 1 data analyst.

## Actions

- Instrumented the full sign-up→first-lesson funnel and found two cliffs: a long pre-lesson setup survey, and a cold "empty dashboard" landing.
- Ran five moderated sessions with new learners — the survey felt like homework before any value.
- Cut the setup survey from nine questions to two and moved the rest into contextual prompts later.
- Replaced the empty dashboard with a single recommended first lesson auto-started on sign-up.
- Shipped it as a 50/50 A/B test over four weeks with first-lesson completion as the primary metric.

## Results and impact

- Week-one first-lesson completion rose from 41% to 63% (+22 points) in the test; shipped to 100%.
- Day-30 retention for the activated cohort improved by 9%.
- The recommended-first-lesson pattern became the default for later engagement work.

## Variants

### Variant: growth angle

---

## Reflection

- What worked: instrumenting first, so the redesign targeted the actual cliffs rather than opinions.
- What I learned: the cheapest activation win was removing steps, not adding guidance.
- What I would do differently: test the survey cut on its own to attribute the lift more cleanly.
- What this says about my strengths/gaps: strong on funnel diagnosis and iteration; the win was incremental, not a 0→1 bet.

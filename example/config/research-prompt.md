# Company Research Brief — Interview Prep Prompt

*(Fictional example — Firstname Lastname. This is the domain-neutral baseline prompt, kept as-is; customise only if your searches need different framing.)*

> **How to use**: Replace `[COMPANY]`, `[ROLE]`, and `[ROLE_TYPE]` before running, then paste the full job ad below the prompt.
> `[ROLE_TYPE]` = a short descriptor of the function or track (e.g. "growth product", "core product").
> The job ad is a required input — the brief cannot be properly calibrated without it.

## Inputs

- The raw job ad for this application
- The company website (offering overview, pricing if applicable, about pages)
- Recent news or funding announcements if available

## Prompt

Use this (or adapt it as needed) when running deep research with an external tool:

---

You are helping a professional prepare for an application and interview process at [COMPANY] for a [ROLE] position. Frame insights for someone on a [ROLE_TYPE] path where relevant.

The job ad is provided below. Treat it as a primary input — cross-reference it throughout to flag where the company's public signals align with or contradict what they say they're looking for.

Produce a concise research brief using the sections below, in order. Focus on concrete signals over generic descriptions. Cite sources. Use these confidence labels consistently: **Source:**, **Estimate:**, **Inferred:**, **Open question:**. Prioritise signal over completeness.

---

### Section 1 — Company and market snapshot (high priority)
### Section 2 — Recent news and key events (medium priority)
### Section 3 — Differentiation and defensibility (very high priority)
### Section 4 — Commercial model and growth levers (high priority)
### Section 5 — Organisation and operating-model signals (high priority)
### Section 6 — Culture and leadership (medium priority)
### Section 7 — People and relationship map (low priority)
### Section 8 — Risks and open questions

---

**Format**: 2–3 page executive briefing. Clear, factual prose. If a point isn't concrete, cut it or flag it as an open question.
